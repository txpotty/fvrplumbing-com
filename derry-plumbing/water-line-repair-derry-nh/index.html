    Water Line Repair We Handle in Derry, NH

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Derry, NH

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Compression fittings there work loose over time. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, because the water provider owns the meter and everything on the street side. If it is ours to fix, we fix it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Derry, NH

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Derry, NH

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patching that kind of pipe tends to buy months, not years. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, usually in one day with a trench or a trenchless pull. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Derry

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. We put a gauge on a hose bib first. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the house shutoff and the meter-side valve for a partly closed gate, and look at the pipe itself. Galvanized pipe can lose half its opening to rust scale. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Derry

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, either along the old route or through fresh soil next to it. Most replacements finish in a day. The new pipe carries its own manufacturer warranty.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Derry

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. Confirming it takes about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and continued movement tells us the leak is in the buried line. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Derry

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in Derry, NH

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Water pouring into a yard undermines everything around it. FVR Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. Most breaks are shut down quickly. If the repair will run long, we rig a temporary bypass so the house has water while we work.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Derry

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. Shallow spot repairs cost the least. Most single-leak repairs land between $300 and $800, while a full main line replacement can run $1,500 to $6,000 or more depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, a wet spot in the yard that stays wet in dry weather, cloudy or rusty water and the sound of running water with every fixture off. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Spot repairs are often exempt. The rules come from your city or county, and they can differ even between neighboring towns in the same county. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, and that coverage usually pays for the line repair. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Mark the pipe route somewhere. Winterizing unused buildings, draining outdoor faucets and keeping heat on avoid most of the freeze damage plumbers see. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Derry, we usually recommend Type K copper or PEX rated for direct burial, or HDPE for a trenchless pull. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Age is the biggest cause. Old pipe corrodes. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, and an old line gets damaged by excavation work more often than most people realize. Poor original installation is another, like rocks dumped right on the pipe.
