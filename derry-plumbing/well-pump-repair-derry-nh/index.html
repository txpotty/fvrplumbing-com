    Constant Pressure System Repair in Derry, NH

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. A fault code on the drive points us in the right direction. The drive, sensor and wiring get tested, and the failed part gets repaired or replaced before we verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Derry, NH

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. Breaker trips usually come first. Licensed technicians test these parts safely with the power locked out, replace what failed and tighten every connection in the box. Wiring meets New Hampshire code when the job is done.

    Jet Pump Repair in Derry, NH

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Lost prime, seal leaks and impeller wear account for most jet pump calls. Our crew in Derry restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for air leaks. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Derry, NH

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. The air charge gets checked and the bladder inspected, then the tank is recharged or replaced so the pump runs a full minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Derry

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. When the bladder fails, the tank fills with water and the pump starts and stops every time someone opens a faucet. The bladder, the switch and the tank fittings all get checked. Proper sizing adds years to a pump.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Derry, NH

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. Our technicians test circuits with the power locked out. All wiring and splices meet New Hampshire electrical code before power goes back on.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Derry

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. Testing starts at the surface. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.
