    Salt-Based Water Softener Systems in Derry, NH

    Salt-based softeners use ion exchange, swapping calcium and magnesium for sodium on a bed of resin beads. FVR Plumbing sets them up with the right resin and brine tanks. We program regeneration by metered water use so the system only recharges when the resin is actually spent, which saves a lot of salt. Most effective option.

    Softener Loop Installation in Derry

    No loop? A loop is the plumbing that routes the main line through a softener, and many older homes were built without one. We cut it in for you. Where we can, we keep hose bibs and irrigation on hard water to save salt. Future service gets easier too.

    Whole-Home Water Softener Installation in Derry

    Whole-home softeners treat every gallon that enters the house, right after the main shutoff. That protects every faucet, shower, water heater and appliance downstream, which is where hard water does most of its damage over the years. We size before we sell. Your test result and water use decide the tank size.

    Water Hardness Testing and Programming in Derry

    Testing is what makes the settings right. Our crew checks hardness, iron and chlorine, then sets the regeneration and salt dosage to match. Iron adds to the setting. We review the settings with you before we leave.

    Water Softener Replacement in Derry, NH

    Older softeners lose ground as resin wears out, valve seals leak or injectors clog. While the old system is out, we inspect the loop and the drain, then install a newer metered unit. Salt use drops. Performance comes back right away.

    Salt-Free Water Conditioner Installation in Derry

    Salt-free conditioners do not soften water, and we say so up front. They change the minerals into crystals that do not stick to pipe walls and heating elements, so scale drops off without adding sodium or using a drain line. Maintenance is light. They suit moderate hardness.

    Water Softener Replacement Services in Derry, NH

    Worn-out softeners usually show it in one of two ways, hard water at the tap or a brine tank that never drops.
