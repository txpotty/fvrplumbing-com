    The pipe wall wears through from the inside. Water chemistry drives it. Aggressive water, high velocity, excess pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues all erode the copper over time until a tiny hole opens. Hot water speeds it up.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. Temporary fixes fail. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, since a failed patch at full pressure can soak a floor in minutes. Use it only until a plumber arrives.

    We typically use Type L copper. It has a thicker wall. Type L lasts longer than the Type M found in many older homes, while press fittings or a PEX transition get used when a torch is a bad idea. All materials are potable-rated.

    Keep water pressure under about 80 psi. A pressure valve does it. Treating hard or aggressive water, insulating pipe in cold spots and limiting how long a recirculation pump runs all reduce the wear that leads to pinholes and failed joints. Find the main valve now.
