    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Claremont

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. Cutting a pit, wiring a new outlet and routing discharge are not, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in Claremont. Hire a licensed plumber for those.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. Digging a new pit and patching the slab can take the whole day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. We tell you up front.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. The job combines electrical, plumbing and concrete work, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Errors appear at the worst moment.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Both move water fine.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    New Hampshire enforces a statewide plumbing code based on the International Plumbing Code, and plumbers are licensed by the state. New pits, new circuits and new discharge routes are the parts most likely to need a plumbing permit. We check the local rules before the job starts, and when a permit applies we pull it and schedule the inspection ourselves. No trips to the permit office.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. New pits and backup systems are a different matter, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Leave those to us.
