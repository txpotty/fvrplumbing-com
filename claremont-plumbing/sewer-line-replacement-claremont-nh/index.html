    What Goes Into a Sewer Line Replacement in Claremont, NH

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Claremont, NH

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. Many mid-century houses got it, and by now most of it has blistered and flattened enough to snag waste. Clay holds up better, except at the joints. Several bad spots on one run usually make full replacement the better buy.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Claremont, NH

    Most of our sewer work is single-family homes. Most homes have basements, so foundation drainage and sump pumps matter for keeping the lower level dry. The usual suspects are rooted clay joints, cast iron worn through at the bottom and sections that sank as the soil settled. A new line keeps sewage from coming up through the basement floor drain or a ground floor shower.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Claremont

    No replacement quote without video. We run a self-leveling color camera from the cleanout to the main and record every joint, crack, root mass and low spot along the way, with footage you keep. A transmitter in the head lets us paint the spot and depth above ground. That one step decides between a spot repair and a full run.

    What Sewer Replacement Costs in Claremont, NH

    Four things set the price: length, depth, access and method. Open trench through an open lawn is usually the cheapest per foot, while bursting costs more per foot but can save thousands in concrete and landscaping repair. Hardscape flips the numbers. After the camera inspection you get a written price for each method that will work.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Claremont

    A winch pulls the bursting head. The head is slightly bigger than the old pipe, so it splits clay or cast iron outward into the soil while fused HDPE trails behind it in one continuous piece. Only two pits get dug. With no joints in the new pipe, roots have no way back in.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Claremont

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Clay joints let roots in every few feet, cast iron scales and rusts through along the bottom, and Orangeburg tar paper pipe slowly flattens into an oval. Modern plastic pipe avoids all three problems. Fused HDPE has no joints at all, so roots have nowhere to start.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Claremont

    Cast iron rots from the inside out. The bottom of a cast iron pipe goes first, and the camera will show a trench rusted through even when the top looks fine. Scale snags paper and grease. We swap the outdoor section for plastic pipe. For cast iron under a slab, we talk through lining versus rerouting.
