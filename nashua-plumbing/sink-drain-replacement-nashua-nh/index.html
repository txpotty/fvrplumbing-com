    PVC is the default choice under most sinks. It will not rust and costs less, while thick brass is worth it where the trap shows, like under a pedestal. ABS works the same way. We match what you have, with a proper transition where they meet.

    Kitchen Sink Drain Replacement in Nashua

    Kitchen drains start failing at the basket strainer. The putty under a 3-1/2 inch strainer dries out, the locknut rusts, and water seeps down the outside of the tailpiece where it looks like the trap is leaking even when it is not. Strainer and trap get swapped as a pair. Disposal sinks get a new flange instead.

    Bathroom Sink Drain Replacement in Nashua, NH

    Bathroom drains wear out at the pop-up. The pivot nut packing leaks, the drain body corrodes around the overflow holes, and the stopper stops sealing, so a sink that will not hold water usually needs a whole new pop-up assembly. Half-fixes do not hold here. The new drain matches your faucet finish.

    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Nashua

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Most glass and stone vessel bowls have no overflow hole, so they need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, and a slotted drain in one of those bowls leaks out of the slots right into the counter hole. We see the wrong drain often. Swapping it takes under an hour.

    Utility and Basement Sink Drain Replacement in Nashua

    Basement sinks often drain to a pump. If your laundry or utility sink sits below the sewer line, its drain runs to a sink pump or a sewage ejector, and we check the check valve and the discharge line when we replace the trap. Pumps fail quietly. A gurgling pit is a clue.

    Bathroom Drain Upgrade in Nashua, NH

    Old bath drains often carry decades of hair and scum. Replacing the pop-up body and the trap removes the rough inside walls that grab hair, and a smooth new 1-1/4 inch path drains a full bowl in seconds instead of minutes. Clogs come back slower. A hair catcher keeps it that way.

    Kitchen Drain and Disposal Tie-In in Nashua

    Disposals change the kitchen drain. The disposal outlet must sit above the trap, the dishwasher hose needs a high loop or air gap, and nothing can pull sideways. We set them all at once. Then the disposal runs with water while we watch.
