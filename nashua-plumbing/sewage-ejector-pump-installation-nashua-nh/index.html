    What Goes Into an Ejector Install in Nashua, NH

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Nashua

    Digging the pit starts with a saw cut through the floor, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Most homes have basements, so foundation drainage and sump pumps matter for keeping the lower level dry. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. A floating basin breaks pipe.

    New Construction Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Nashua, NH

    On a new build, the basin goes in before the slab is poured. That is the cheapest time, since there is no concrete to cut, and we stub the drains, the vent and the discharge to the plans so the rough-in inspection goes smoothly. Builders like the timing.

    Laundry and Wet Bar Ejector Pumps in Nashua, NH

    Below-grade laundry rooms and wet bars send gray water down that still needs to be lifted to the sewer. A compact gray water pump handles it, sized for the 15 or more gallons a washer drops at once, with a lint filter to keep fibers off the impeller. Lint kills small pumps.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Nashua, NH

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. With a sound basin, a new pump, fresh check valve, shutoff and alarm give you most of a new system for far less. Cracked basins get replaced.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Nashua

    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. We still add a shutoff.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Nashua

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Nashua

    Macerating systems like Saniflo let you add a toilet and sink without breaking the concrete floor. It sits behind the toilet, grinds waste and pumps it through a small line, often three-quarter or one inch, to a drain above, which suits a small half bath. Venting still applies.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Nashua

    Zoeller's sewage line runs from light residential to heavy duty, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. The model comes from the pump curve for your lift, and most basement baths need a four-tenths or half horsepower unit that passes two-inch solids. Tall lifts need bigger pumps.
