    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Nashua

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. The carrier itself sometimes needs new bolts or a coupling. We match the new bowl to the existing carrier whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Toilet Replacement Cost Comparison in Nashua, NH

    Three repair calls in a year on the same toilet usually cost more than replacing it, especially when the toilet is old enough to waste water on every flush. You get repair and replacement prices next to each other. You make the call, and we do not push replacement on a toilet that only needs a flapper.

    Toilet Rough-In Adjustments in Nashua, NH

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, usually 12 inches, sometimes 10 or 14 in older homes. Take the measurement from the wall to the closet bolts, not from the baseboard trim. If the new toilet's rough-in does not match, it either sits too far out or will not fit. An offset flange can correct an inch or two without moving the drain.

    High-Efficiency Toilet (HET) Upgrades in Nashua, NH

    High-efficiency toilets carry a WaterSense label and a MaP score, which tells you how many grams of waste the toilet clears in one flush. Look for 800 or higher. Higher numbers mean fewer double flushes and fewer plunger trips, and that is where the real water savings come from.

    Gravity-Flush Toilet Replacement in Nashua

    Gravity-flush toilets are the simplest and most forgiving, and a well-made one with a 3 inch flush valve will outflush most of the older toilets it replaces. Replacement parts are on every hardware shelf. We set it level with shims if the floor is out, then caulk the base and test several full flushes.

    Cracked Bowl or Tank Replacement in Nashua

    Sometimes only the tank is cracked, and if the model is still in production, a matching tank costs less than a whole new toilet. We check the model number first. A cracked bowl always means a full replacement, since the bowl carries the seal to the floor and cannot be patched.

    Water-Saving Toilet Upgrades in Nashua, NH

    Older toilets are one of the largest indoor water users in a house, and replacing a 3.5 gallon model with a 1.28 gallon one cuts toilet water use by more than half. Dual-flush toilets add a lighter flush for liquids. We recommend models with high MaP scores so the savings do not come with double flushing.

    Commercial Toilet Replacement in Nashua, NH

    Office and retail restrooms wear out toilets faster than any house, and a replacement there usually means a floor-mount or wall-hung bowl with a flushometer instead of a tank. We schedule around your hours. Bowls are matched to the flush valve's gallons per flush, since a mismatch leaves waste behind or wastes water.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Nashua

    Expect roughly $350 to $800 for a standard, chair height or high-efficiency toilet installed, with the old one removed and hauled away. Rusted flanges and tight spaces push it higher. You get a firm number up front.

    About two hours for a standard swap. That covers removal, flange inspection, setting the new toilet and testing it, with the old fixture loaded on the truck at the end. Flange repairs add time.

    A pro install costs more up front, but it comes with a flange check, the right closet bolts and a warranty if the seal leaks later. A DIY seal that leaks can quietly rot the subfloor. Fixing that costs much more than paying for the install.

    Most swaps take 1.5 to 2.5 hours. Flange or subfloor repairs can add an hour.

    A like-for-like replacement in the same spot generally does not. Moving drains or adding a bathroom usually does.

    TOTO, American Standard, Kohler and Gerber all make good toilets, and we steer people toward the models in each line with a 3 inch flush valve and a strong MaP score. Flush design matters more than the badge. We also weigh how easy the parts are to find.

    A handle that needs jiggling, a tank that refills on its own, a hiss after the flush or a flush that stops halfway all point to worn tank parts. Those can be replaced one at a time. On a toilet past 25 years, replacing the whole fixture often makes more sense.

    Watch for cracks, weekly clogs, a base that rocks after it has been reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. The age of the toilet matters too. Toilets past 25 years old are often worth replacing even if nothing is broken yet.
