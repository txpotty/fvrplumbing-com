    Rheem Gas Water Heater Service in Nashua, NH

    Rheem tanks and their Ruud twins share most parts, which helps when a gas valve fails a few years in. We install both. Setup follows the factory manual, including the draft test.

    Rheem Gas Water Heater Replacement in Nashua, NH

    Rheem Performance and Performance Platinum tanks cover most of the range we install, from basic 6-year models to 12-year tanks with better anodes. More warranty years generally buy a heavier anode rod. Choosing is simple.

    Propane Gas Water Heater Replacement in Nashua, NH

    Propane heaters run at about 11 inches of water column instead of the 7 inches typical for natural gas, so the gas valve and orifice have to match the fuel. We never swap a natural gas tank onto propane without the right conversion. Pressure gets checked with a manometer.

    No Hot Water Calls in Nashua

    No hot water at all usually points to a dead thermocouple or a gas control valve that has failed after years of service. Those get tested before anything else. On a tank past ten years, a new heater usually beats a new valve.

    Atmospheric Vent Upgrades in Nashua, NH

    Flue pipes on older atmospheric heaters rust at the joints and lose their upward slope, and a loose connector can spill exhaust into the closet. We replace damaged sections and the draft hood if needed. Then we test draft with the burner on.

    Bradford White Tank Replacement in Nashua

    Most of the tanks on our trucks are Bradford White, partly because their Defender combustion system and ICON gas valve are simple to service. We help you match model to demand. The install meets current code.

    Power Vent Water Heater Replacement in Nashua

    Blower motors and pressure switches are the weak points on power vent tanks. A second blower failure on an older tank is usually the point where a new heater makes more sense. We confirm a dedicated outlet first.

    Atmospheric Vent Gas Water Heater Replacement in Nashua

    Atmospheric vent heaters last 10 to 12 years on average, and the end usually shows up as rusty hot water or a slow drip from the bottom. We disconnect the old unit and reconnect the new one to the existing gas line and flue. Combustion air matters. We confirm a closet install has louvers or ducted air before we light the burner.
