    Barrier-Free Walk-In Showers in Nashua, NH

    Barrier-free entries need careful drain placement. We work with your tile setter on the waterproofing membrane and the slope, since water has to reach the drain before it can roll out the open side of the shower. Humid summers bring sweating on cold water lines and damp basements that can hide slow leaks. We pick the pan system to match.

    Shower Upgrades Without a Full Remodel in Nashua

    Not every upgrade needs demolition. A new pan, a tub conversion or a new valve and trim can change the bathroom without a gut job. We price the options side by side. Most of these finish in a few days.

    Commercial Shower Installation in Nashua, NH

    Locker rooms need showers that take hundreds of uses a day. Commercial installs get tamper-resistant trim and heads, plus drains sized for heavy simultaneous use. We work around your business. A master mixing valve can protect the whole room from scalding.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Nashua

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. Valve, head, handheld and drain go in first, then the pan is sloped a quarter inch per foot to the drain. Then we test. The valve gets a pressure test and the pan a 24-hour flood test before tile.

    Steam Shower Plumbing in Nashua

    Steam showers need four things: a generator, a water supply, a drain line and an electrical circuit. The generator goes within the maker's distance limit, and we plumb its supply, drain and steam line to the head. An electrician runs the circuit. A sealed enclosure with a sloped ceiling keeps drips off you.

    Budget Shower Upgrades in Nashua, NH

    Budget showers can still be built right. A fiberglass or acrylic unit with a quality pressure-balance valve costs far less than a tiled shower, and when it is set on a mortar bed with a sealed drain, it lasts decades. Spend on the valve. You get the full price before we start.

    Curbless Shower Installation in Nashua

    Curbless showers need the floor lowered in the shower area. On a wood floor, we drop the joists or use a recessed pan system, and on a slab, we cut and lower the concrete so the finished floor runs flat into the shower. A linear drain often works best. Slope and waterproofing get planned before demo.

    Shower Pan Installation in Nashua

    Shower pans fail quietly. A pan with a pinhole in the liner or a poorly clamped drain will hold water for years while it slowly soaks the subfloor, so we flood-test every tiled pan for 24 hours before tile goes down. We level prefab pans carefully. Both kinds get slope toward the drain.
