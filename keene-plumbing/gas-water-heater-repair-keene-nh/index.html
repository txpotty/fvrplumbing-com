    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Keene

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. The control's error code is our starting point. Every suspect part then gets tested with a meter.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Problems in Keene, NH

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. A lot of the time it is the drain valve, the T&P valve or a dripping supply fitting. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Keene, NH home. Water coming from the bottom seam means the tank itself is done. Nothing patches that.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Keene

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water and years without a flush both make it worse. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Keene

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.
