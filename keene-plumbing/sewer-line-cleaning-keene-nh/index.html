    Scheduled cleaning beats emergency calls. Grease builds again. A regular cleaning schedule keeps roots and grease in check, especially on older lines near big trees.

    Sewer Camera Inspections in Keene

    Repeat problems call for a camera. Video ends the guessing. We look inside the line to see what is causing the trouble, then choose the cheapest fix that will hold.

    Sewer Line Camera Inspection in Keene

    A camera shows the line in real time. Bellies and cracks show up. We look for low spots where water pools, offset joints, cracks, roots and corrosion, and a locator in the camera head lets us mark the exact spot and depth on the surface, with a copy of the video for you.

    Routine Sewer Line Cleaning in Keene

    Routine cleaning clears slow buildup. It narrows the line over time. Our tech runs a cable from the cleanout with a blade sized for the line, then jets the walls if needed, and most older lines do well with cleaning every one to two years.

    Sewer Line Odor Remediation in Keene, NH

    Sewer smells mean something is wrong. Gas may be escaping. Persistent odors from drains or the yard often come from trapped waste, a blockage or a damaged line letting gas escape, and our cleaning removes the source while the camera finds any break.

    Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Keene, NH

    Jetting cleans the pipe wall. A snake punches a hole. High-pressure water strips grease, scale and fine roots off the inside of the line and flushes them toward the street, and with the right settings it is safe for PVC and older clay alike.

    Residential Sewer Line Cleaning in Keene

    Household laterals collect grease and hair. Both build up slowly. Kitchen grease and bathroom hair stick to rough spots and joints until the 4 inch line narrows enough to back up, and older cast iron makes it worse by adding scale for debris to grab, and we clear it with a cable or a jetter sized for the pipe.

    Urgent Sewer Backups in Keene

    Raw sewage in the house is an emergency. It gets top priority. An emergency tech clears the line, contains the mess and photographs the damage for insurance.
