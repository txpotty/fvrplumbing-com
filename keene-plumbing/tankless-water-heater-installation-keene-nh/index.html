    Why Choose FVR Plumbing for Tankless Water Heater Installation in Keene, NH?

    Tankless heaters cost more up front than tanks, so getting the install right matters. A unit with a short-cycling problem from bad sizing or a vent that ices over will spend its life throwing error codes, and the homeowner pays for that every month. We size by the numbers.

    • Accurate Sizing, We size by gallons per minute and incoming water temperature.
    • Lower Gas Use, High-efficiency units turn more of the gas into hot water.
    • Replaceable Parts, Parts like sensors and fans can be replaced without replacing the whole unit.

    Citywide Coverage

    Older central neighborhoods and newer subdivisions in Keene both get our full tankless service. Point-of-use electric units for a distant bath, garage sink or basement bar are part of the work too, fixing a long wait without touching the main heater. Those take a couple of hours.

    Experienced Team

    Our team uses the code tables to size gas piping, not by rule of thumb. Pipe length from the meter, the number of fittings and every other gas appliance in the house all affect what size line the tankless unit needs to run at full fire without starving the furnace. Starved units throw codes.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Keene

    Gas tankless units in Keene are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature.     Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Keene, NH

    Calcium scale is what kills most tankless units early. Testing well water around Keene is the right first step before choosing a neutralizer or filter. Once scale coats the heat exchanger, efficiency drops and error codes start, and over time it can crack the exchanger and void the warranty. A yearly flush prevents it. We install isolation valves for that.

    Cost-Effective Tankless Conversions in Keene, NH

    A tankless conversion in Keene, NH costs more than a tank swap, mostly because of the gas line upgrade, the new venting and the unit price itself. The savings come back over time. With no standby loss and a lifespan that can reach 20 years, many homeowners see the difference on the gas bill within the first few winters. Your quote shows the math.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Keene, NH

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. The vent changes as well. Both are on the estimate.
