    Picking a Tankless for Your Rochester, NH House

    Tankless vs. Tank Replacement Cost in Rochester, NH

    Tankless costs more up front than a tank, often twice as much installed. Across 20 years the gap narrows, because one tankless may outlast two tanks, but your usage decides the payback. You see both quotes.

    Exterior Mount Tankless Units in Rochester, NH

    Exterior-mount tankless units hang on an outside wall and vent straight out the front, so there is no vent pipe to run. Covers and wiring take a beating outdoors. Winterizing unused buildings, draining outdoor faucets and keeping heat on prevent most of the freeze damage plumbers see. On every outdoor swap, we check freeze protection and the pipe cover. Often the simplest job we do.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Rochester

    Sizing a tankless starts with how many fixtures run at once and how cold the incoming water is, not the square footage of the house. Two showers and a dishwasher at the same time can need 7 or 8 gallons per minute. The unit is chosen from that figure. Any gas, water or vent changes the new unit needs get done to current code.

    Point-of-Use Electric Tankless in Rochester

    Point-of-use electric tankless heaters under a sink are a different animal from whole-house units. They run on a single 20 or 30 amp circuit and work well for a far bathroom or an office sink. Most take under two hours.

    Rinnai Condensing Upgrades in Rochester, NH

    Rinnai Sensei condensing models replace older non-condensing Rinnai units in a lot of homes, but the venting changes from stainless to PVC. We handle that conversion. Most of the plumbing lines up already.

    Condensing Tankless Replacement in Rochester

    Condensing tankless units like the Navien NPE or Rinnai Sensei pass 90 percent efficiency by pulling extra heat out of the exhaust. They vent through PVC. Acidic condensate goes through a neutralizer and then to an approved drain. Long, cold winters are the main stress on plumbing in New Hampshire, and the heating season can last for many months. Showers and laundry run all year, so the gas savings do too.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Rochester, NH

    Pulling a 50 gallon tank out of a garage frees up floor space and ends standby heat loss. The catch is gas, since most tankless units need a 3/4-inch line or bigger plus new venting. Before quoting, we check the meter and pipe run, and afterward we cap the old flue and seal the roof. Expect a full day.

    Like-for-Like Tankless Swaps in Rochester

    A cracked heat exchanger or a failed control board on an older tankless is usually not worth repairing past the ten-year mark. The replacement gets matched to the existing gas, vent and mount where possible. Plan on four to six hours.
