    Where We Find Leaks in Rochester, NH

    Pool Leak Detection in Rochester

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation likely has a leak somewhere. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, use an underwater hydrophone to listen, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain to find the exact crack or fitting that is losing water.

    Pool and Spa Leak Detection in Rochester

    Heat and wind can pull a lot of water out of a pool, so it is hard to tell evaporation from a leak. A bucket test settles it. We then pressure test the plumbing lines and dye test around lights, skimmers and return fittings, since a leak left alone can wash out the soil behind the shell.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Rochester, NH

    A ceiling stain right below a bathroom usually points to a shower valve, a drain joint or a toilet wax ring. Our job is figuring out which. Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe before any big opening is made.

    Leak Testing for Multifamily and Offices in Rochester

    Multifamily buildings and office parks have long supply systems that let leaks hide for months. We isolate each unit or floor, test it and trace leaks with the same acoustic and thermal tools we use in single-family houses. Every unit gets its own report. Early detection means fewer tenant complaints and fewer insurance claims building-wide.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection in Rochester, NH

    A cracked drain line leaks wastewater into the soil, which can show up as a sewer smell in the yard, a soggy patch or slow drains. We use a camera for these. The camera goes in at the cleanout and runs to the main, recording cracks, offset joints and root entry in PVC, clay and cast iron, with a locator giving the exact depth and distance.

    Slab Leak Detection in Rochester

    Concrete slab homes have supply lines buried in sand below the floor, and a pinhole there can run for months before anyone notices. Look for warm tile first. We pressure test the hot and cold sides separately, then listen through the slab with a ground microphone and confirm with a thermal camera when the leak is on the hot side.

    Main Water Line Leak Detection in Rochester

    Soggy grass near the front walk or a sinking spot over the line can mean the service pipe is leaking. Old galvanized and copper lines fail most. We map the pipe with a line tracer and listen for the leak with a ground microphone, which keeps the dig to a single hole rather than a trench. Repair options follow.

    Underground Water Line Leak Detection in Rochester, NH

    The buried line from your meter to the house can leak for months without surfacing, especially in old galvanized pipe. Green grass in a dry spell is a clue. We trace the line with an electronic locator, then use a ground microphone to hear the leak, switching to tracer gas on plastic lines that do not carry sound.
