    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Rochester, NH

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Rochester

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Rochester, NH

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. The control box capacitor and relay are next on the list. We lock out the power before testing anything. All wiring and splices meet New Hampshire electrical code before power goes back on.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Rochester, NH

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Nor'easters and tropical storm remnants can bring heavy rain that overwhelms drainage and floods low-lying areas. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. An arrestor costs little.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Rochester

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. Common causes include a plugged sediment filter, a low switch setting or a yard line leak. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Control Box & Electrical Repair in Rochester, NH

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets New Hampshire code before we restart the pump.

    No-Water Same-Day Repair in Rochester

    A dead pump leaves the house without toilets, showers or drinking water. FVR Plumbing answers emergency well pump calls throughout Rochester and nearby areas ahead of scheduled jobs. Power and the pressure switch get checked first. Those two items fix a surprising share of no-water calls without anyone having to touch the pump hanging down in the well.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Rochester

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. Testing starts at the surface. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.
