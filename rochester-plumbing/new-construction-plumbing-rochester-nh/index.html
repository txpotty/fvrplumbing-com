    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Rochester

    Most new single-family homes in the Rochester area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Tankless heaters and gas runs add to it. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It flexes and needs fewer fittings in the walls than copper. Drains and vents are usually Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, depending on what the local code allows and what the plans specify for the building. We still use copper near water heaters.

    You can. Plenty of owners choose fixtures at a supply house showroom. We need the model numbers before rough-in, because shower valves, freestanding tub fillers and wall-mount faucets all need a specific rough-in body set at the right depth. Swapping valve brands after drywall often means cutting the wall.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drain, waste and vent runs are PVC or ABS. Cast iron is still used in some multifamily buildings and in walls next to bedrooms where drain noise matters, since it is much quieter than plastic when a toilet flushes upstairs.

    A local plumber knows which inspector wants the test gauge at the front door and which wants it at the manifold. That sounds minor. It is the difference between passing on the morning visit and getting a correction notice that pushes your drywall date back two days. Utility tap rules are familiar ground too.

    Most homes have basements, so foundation drainage and sump pumps matter for keeping the lower level dry. Ground movement puts stress on pipes under a slab. We bed drain lines in sand or gravel, sleeve every pipe that passes through concrete, and use flexible PEX for the water service where code allows it. Sleeves are cheap.

    Plumbing is usually covered under the general building permit. Where a separate plumbing permit is required, we pull it in our name as the licensed contractor. Either way, we schedule the plumbing inspections, attend them, and handle any corrections without the builder having to chase us down about it.

    Scheduling causes most of the trouble. A late pour or a framing change can stack plumbing phases on top of each other.
