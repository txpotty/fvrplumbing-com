    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Manchester

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. Less water goes down the drain.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Services in Manchester, NH

    Businesses in Manchester, NH with steady hot water use, like salons, restaurants and small gyms, often get more out of two or three tankless units racked together than one big tank. If one unit goes down, the others keep running. We size the rack by peak demand. Expect a bigger gas line and a mechanical permit on commercial jobs.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Manchester, NH

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Water in much of New Hampshire is soft and often slightly acidic, which can gradually wear down copper pipe and cause pinhole leaks. Once scale coats the heat exchanger, efficiency drops and error codes start, and over time it can crack the exchanger and void the warranty. Flush it yearly. Service valves make it easy.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Manchester, NH

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Manchester, NH homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Manchester

    Propane homes in Manchester can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Since propane runs at different pressure from natural gas, the regulator, line sizing and gas valve must all match, and the supplier may need to swap the tank regulator. Tank size matters too.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Manchester, NH

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. The vent changes as well. We plan both on the quote.
