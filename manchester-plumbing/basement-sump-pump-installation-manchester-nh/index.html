    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Manchester, NH

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. Spring snowmelt and heavy rain can raise groundwater quickly in New Hampshire, sending water into basements that stay dry most of the year. A second pump goes into the same pit on its own float switch, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. The main pump keeps doing the daily work.

    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Manchester

    Older homes often have narrow pits that will not fit a submersible. That is where a pedestal pump earns its place, and we install it with rigid discharge, a quality check valve, and a float rod set to your pit depth so it starts before water reaches the top of the liner. Noisy, but they last.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Manchester

    Rigid PVC carries the water from the pump out through the rim joist and at least 10 feet from the house. A spring check valve stops the column from falling back into the pit, which cuts motor run time and the loud thunk you hear after every cycle. Winterizing unused buildings, draining outdoor faucets and keeping heat on avoid most of the freeze damage plumbers see. We fit a freeze-resistant outlet.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Manchester

    No pit yet? We find the low spot where water collects, saw cut the slab, dig roughly 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a liner surrounded by washed gravel so water can enter while fine soil stays out. New concrete goes back flush and level.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Manchester, NH

    Submersible units sit down in the pit, sealed, with the water around them cooling the motor. That makes them quieter than a pedestal, and in Manchester, NH we set them on a couple of bricks so the intake stays above the silt that settles on the bottom of every liner. The cord seal matters.

    Primary Sump Pump Replacement in Manchester

    Pumps wear out, usually somewhere between seven and ten years.     Some lots sit in a high water table and fill a standard pit every few minutes during spring. For those, we step up to a 1/2 or 3/4 horsepower unit in a bigger basin, all sized after we time how fast your pit refills. We use a stopwatch.
