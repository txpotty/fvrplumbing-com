    What Goes Into a Repipe in Manchester, NH

    PEX Pipe Installation in Manchester

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster. Scale and rust do not affect it. Testing well water around Manchester is the right first step before choosing a neutralizer or filter. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings or PEX-b with crimp rings depending on the job, choosing a layout that fits the house. Fewer joints, fewer leaks.

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Manchester

    A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, including the water heater connections. Most single-story homes take two to three days. We run new trunk lines through the attic or crawlspace, branch down to fixtures through small wall openings, and keep water on at night whenever we can. Then patching.

    Burst Pipe Repair and Replacement in Manchester, NH

    Winterizing unused buildings, draining outdoor faucets and keeping heat on avoid most of the freeze damage plumbers see. Frozen pipes split when water inside expands. Exposed pipe in crawlspaces, garages and outside walls is most at risk, so we fix it and protect it from the next freeze. Then we test it.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Manchester, NH

    Copper still has fans. It handles heat well and lasts decades. We install Type L copper for most interior supply lines, sweat or press the joints, while strapping each line properly, so the new pipe does not rattle or sag. Costs more than PEX.

    Full Repipes for Older Homes in Manchester

    Homes from the 1950s and 1960s often still have original galvanized pipe. It is near the end. A full repipe brings new PEX or copper to every fixture, improves pressure, clears up discolored water, and removes the leak risk from aging threads. Worth it.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Manchester, NH

    We often leave old galvanized pipe in the wall. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, which keeps drywall damage low. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Manchester, NH

    Galvanized steel was common before the 1960s. Inside, rust builds once the coating fails. Over decades the opening shrinks, pressure drops and the water turns brown after sitting, while threaded joints corrode through first. Replacing it is the only fix.

    Corroded Pipe Replacement in Manchester, NH

    Corrosion shows up as green or white crust on copper, rust on steel, or pinholes that drip. Some water is harder on pipe. We replace corroded sections or the whole system depending on how widespread the damage is, and we check the water heater connections and shutoffs since they often corrode too. Look at the joints.
