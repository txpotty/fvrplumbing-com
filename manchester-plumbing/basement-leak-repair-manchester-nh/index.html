    New Sump Pump Installation in Manchester

    Installing a new sump means breaking out a section of slab, setting a lined pit, and running the discharge out through the rim joist.     Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Manchester

    Price depends on the source. A supply line or fitting repair usually runs a few hundred dollars. Crack injection, sump replacement and interior drain systems step up from there, with full perimeter drains or exterior waterproofing reaching several thousand dollars because of the concrete work and digging.     Heavy storms dump a lot of water quickly, and the soil around the foundation cannot absorb it fast enough. Nor'easters and tropical storm remnants can bring intense rain that overwhelms drainage and floods low-lying areas. Saturated soil pushes water against the walls and up under the slab through the cove joint, so the leak shows only when the ground is soaked. Dry months hide it.

    Small repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger work is different. Stack replacement and new backwater valves often need one, and we handle the permit and inspection.

    Usually not, unless the leak is at an exposed fitting you can reach. A loose compression nut or a worn washer hose is fine to fix yourself. Soldering overhead, cutting cast iron or chasing a hidden leak takes tools most homeowners do not have. Guessing gets expensive.

    Plumbing leaks show up no matter the weather. Groundwater leaks track the rain. A moving meter with everything off means a pipe leak, and water that shows up after storms means groundwater.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Wet cracks get polyurethane. Our interior drains use rigid perforated pipe, which keeps its slope and can be jetted later.

    Leak repair stops a specific source, like a cracked pipe or a leaking wall crack. Waterproofing covers the whole wall. It handles groundwater all around the foundation and makes sense when water comes in at many points.

    Look for damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell, and white chalky deposits on concrete. Those deposits are efflorescence, left behind when water evaporates.
