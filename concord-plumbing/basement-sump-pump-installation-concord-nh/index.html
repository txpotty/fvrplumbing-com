    The Sump Pump Work We Do in Concord, NH

    Residential Basement Sump Pump Installation in Concord

    A typical house pit holds an 18 by 22 inch basin with a 1/3 horsepower pump, and that covers most homes. Our plumbers size the unit to the water coming in and the height it has to climb, not to the sale sticker. In Concord, we set the basin at the low corner, bed it in washed gravel and run 1-1/2 inch PVC out through the rim joist. Discharge gets carried far enough out that it cannot soak back toward the wall.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Concord, NH

    Submersible units sit down in the pit, sealed, with the water around them cooling the motor. That makes them quieter than a pedestal, and in Concord, NH we set them on a couple of bricks so the intake stays above the silt that settles on the bottom of every liner. Sealed wiring matters here.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Concord

    No pit yet? We find the low spot where water collects, saw cut the slab, dig roughly 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a liner surrounded by washed gravel so water can enter while fine soil stays out. We pour new concrete flush with the floor.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Concord, NH

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. A battery backup sump pump and a backwater valve are worthwhile protection for any finished basement. We add a second pump in the same pit with its own float, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. The main pump keeps doing the daily work.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Concord

    Rigid PVC carries the water from the pump out through the rim joist and at least 10 feet from the house. We add a spring check valve so the water in the pipe cannot fall back, which cuts motor run time and the loud thunk you hear after every cycle. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, camps and seasonal homes are especially likely to freeze when the heat is turned down. We fit a freeze-resistant outlet.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Concord

    Duplex systems with two pumps and an alternating panel are standard for parking garages and multi-unit buildings with below-grade levels in Concord. The panel swaps the lead pump every cycle, so wear stays even. Staff get a high water alarm. We coordinate the wiring with your electrician.

    Zoeller Sump Pump Installation in Concord

    Zoeller's M53 Mighty-Mate is the cast iron pump we install more than any other. It is a 3/10 horsepower unit with a vertical float switch, and it moves about 2,580 gallons an hour at 5 feet of lift, enough for a lot of Concord basements. Replacement switches are on every supply shelf.

    Emergency Sump Pump Installation in Concord, NH

    A dead pump with water rising in the pit is a same-day call. We send a plumber with a cast iron pump on the truck, pulls the old unit, checks whether the float or the check valve caused the failure, and gets water moving again in Concord, NH. Bail with a bucket until we arrive.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Concord

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Concord. At the low end you get a pump swap in a pit that is already there, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. We quote after seeing it.

    Liberty Pumps, Zoeller and Wayne are our regulars. Each offers cast iron housings and dependable switches, and all three have solid parts support through local supply houses, which matters more than a fancy feature list when something breaks. We match the model to your pit.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. New pits and backup systems are a different matter, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Leave those to us.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. The real answer depends on inflow and lift, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Dump a bucket of water into the pit slowly, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. It takes a minute.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Parts for all four are easy to find at supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. Common models ride on our trucks.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Either type gets the job done.

    A battery backup sump pump and a backwater valve are smart protection for any finished basement. Outages and storms arrive together, so we recommend a battery backup in most basements. The backup starts on its own if the main pump has no power, and it also catches the water if the primary switch sticks or the pump simply cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. It is cheap insurance.
