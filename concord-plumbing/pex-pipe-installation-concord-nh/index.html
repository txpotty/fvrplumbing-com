    What Goes Into a PEX Job in Concord, NH

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Concord, NH

    With PEX-A, the tech slips an expansion ring onto the pipe and stretches both with a battery tool. The fitting goes in while the plastic is still expanded, and the material's memory pulls it back down around the barb, sealing harder over the first minute. Cold weather slows it. Joints get time to set before we pressurize.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Concord

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Concord

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Concord

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating work calls for oxygen-barrier PEX, coated so oxygen cannot seep through the pipe wall and corrode the boiler, the circulator and the iron parts. Loop length matters as well. Run them too long and the last stretch never warms up.
