    Urgent Softener Problems in Concord

    Not every softener failure is urgent. A unit leaking from the valve body or the brine tank overflowing onto the floor is, and we move those calls up the schedule. Turn the bypass handle and wait for us.

    Brine Tank Replacement in Concord

    Salt bridges, crusty layers of salt that form over a hollow, stop the brine from making contact with the water and the softener quits softening. We break them up and clean out the mush at the bottom. Cracked tanks and stuck safety floats get replaced. Use pellet salt.

    Salt-Free Alternatives in Concord, NH

    Some homeowners do not want salt or have no drain near the main line. In those cases a TAC conditioner works, though we are upfront that it stops scale and does not make water feel soft. Sodium concerns are another reason.

    Metered Softener Replacement in Concord

    Timer softeners regenerate every few days whether you used 50 gallons or 500. A metered valve, Fleck 5600SXT or Clack WS1, tracks gallons and regenerates when the resin actually needs it. Most people notice less salt in a few weeks.

    Dual Tank Softener Systems in Concord

    Twin-tank systems keep one tank in service while the other regenerates, so you never get hard water during a middle-of-the-night regeneration. Kinetico's version uses water pressure, not power, to switch tanks. The price is higher.

    Softener Loop Installation in Concord, NH

    Softener loops are the pair of stubbed-out lines near the garage wall or water heater where a softener ties into the main. Homes without one get a loop cut in, with shutoffs and a bypass. Hose bibs stay off it. The drain goes to a laundry box or floor drain with an air gap.

    Commercial Softener Replacement in Concord

    Espresso machines and commercial dishwashers scale up fast on hard water, and the service bills for descaling them are steep. We size commercial softeners to flow rate and daily gallons. A carbon prefilter may go first.

    Softener Replacement on a Budget in Concord, NH

    Capacity and valve brand set most of the price. For a small house on moderately hard water, a basic metered single-tank unit often does the job for much less than a twin-tank system. We show both options side by side.
