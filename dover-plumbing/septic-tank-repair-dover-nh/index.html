    Septic Tank Fixes We Handle in Dover, NH

    Septic Line Clog Clearing in Dover

    A slow toilet and a gurgle in the tub usually point to a clog between the house and the tank. We run a camera down the line first, then clear it with a jetter set up for a 4-inch building sewer. Grease and wipes cause most of these, and the fix after clearing depends on which one we pull out. Roots mean a joint has opened.

    Effluent Pump Replacement in Dover, NH

    Effluent pumps in a dosing chamber last roughly 8 to 15 years, and they usually quit without much warning at all. A pump that hums but moves no water has a stuck impeller or a bad capacitor. We pull it and test the discharge line before setting a new one. Floats get replaced at the same time.

    Septic Tee Repair in Dover

    Sanitary tees on the inlet and outlet can crack or get knocked off by the hose on a pump truck. Without the outlet tee, the floating scum layer drifts into the laterals and plugs the gravel. We glue in a new Schedule 40 tee. An effluent filter goes on if the outlet has room.

    House-to-Tank Line Repair in Dover

    Gurgling drains right after a pump-out point to the line between the house and the tank, not the tank itself. Roots and settling ground cause most sags and breaks in that run. A camera from the house cleanout finds the bad joint, and we mark it in the yard. Then we dig one hole, not a trench. A second cleanout near the tank goes in while it is open.

    Septic Code and Permit Work in Dover, NH

    New tanks and new fields need a permit from the local health authority in most of the country, along with any change to the system's layout.     Septic Lid and Riser Repair in Dover

    Old concrete lids crack, and a cracked lid over a 1,000-gallon tank is a real danger to kids and pets. We replace failing lids and add risers that bring access up to the lawn. Future pumping gets cheaper. Every lid we set is screwed down or locked so a child cannot lift it.

    Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Dover, NH

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones get eaten by sulfuric acid from sewer gas and eventually fall off into the tank. Once the outlet baffle is gone, solids head straight for the field. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.
