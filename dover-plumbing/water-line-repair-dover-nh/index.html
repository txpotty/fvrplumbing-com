    What Our Water Line Service Covers in Dover, NH

    Main Water Line Repair in Dover

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Dover, NH

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. Shutting the water off comes first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Same-day repairs are the norm. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Dover

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Dover

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Dover, NH

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Dover

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. Confirming it takes about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and if it keeps creeping we know the leak is between the meter and the house. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Dover

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, either along the old route or through fresh soil next to it. Most replacements finish in a day. The new pipe carries its own manufacturer warranty.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Dover, NH

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patches on those materials rarely hold. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, usually in one day with a trench or a trenchless pull. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Resulting damage sometimes is. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, and that coverage usually pays for the line repair. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Copper is Type K for buried runs. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, and we stick with fittings rated for direct burial in the soil conditions we find. We do not use cheap knockoff fittings.

    A large share of homes rely on private wells, and some wells carry naturally occurring arsenic, radon or iron that call for treatment.     Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    Fix small leaks early. Little leaks grow. Check your pressure once a year, because a bad regulator stresses every pipe in the house, and keep big-rooted trees off the pipe route. For older homes in Dover, replacing an aging galvanized line on your own schedule usually costs less than an emergency.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, from locating the leak to filling the hole. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.
