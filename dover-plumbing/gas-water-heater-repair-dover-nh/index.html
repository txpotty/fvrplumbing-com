    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Dover, NH

    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Dover

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Dover

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in Dover, NH

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. Plenty of leaks come from the drain valve, the relief valve or a sweating supply nipple. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Dover, NH home. Water coming from the bottom seam means the tank itself is done. No patch fixes that.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Dover

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. A high whistle is different. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Dover

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Then each part gets a meter test.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Dover, NH

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. We check the thermostat setting, the immersion sensor and the valve's outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. Every joint on the new valve gets leak tested.

    Gas Water Heater Code Checks in Dover, NH

    Fuel gas code covers more of a water heater repair than most people expect. We check for a sediment trap, an approved flex connector and a shutoff valve within reach of the unit. Garage installs in Dover, NH need the burner raised 18 inches off the floor unless the heater is listed as vapor resistant, and strapping goes on wherever the local code asks for it. We measure clearances as well.
