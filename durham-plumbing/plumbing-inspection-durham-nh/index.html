    Preventive Maintenance Checks in Durham, NH

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems.     Water Heater Safety Inspection in Durham

    A water heater stores a lot of hot water under pressure, so safety items matter. We test the T and P valve, confirm its discharge line runs to a safe place, look for an expansion tank where one is needed and check the drain pan. Gas units get a CO check. A heater that backdrafts can spill combustion gases into the house, so we test the draft at the vent hood and note any problems right away.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Durham

    A standard plumbing inspection in Durham, NH usually costs $150 to $350. Size changes the price. That fee covers the supply pipe, fixtures, water heater, drains and pressure check, while a sewer camera run is usually priced as an add-on because it takes extra time and equipment. We quote it on the phone.

    Every one to two years works for most homes. Older homes may need more. If your home has original supply pipe, large trees near the sewer or a history of leaks, a yearly inspection catches problems while they are still small and cheap to fix.

    We identify supply pipe materials including polybutylene, Kitec, galvanized, copper, CPVC and PEX. We note known risks and replacement options.

    It is smart before buying any home, especially one over 25 years old or with large trees. Repeated backups or gurgling drains are other good reasons.

    Rising water bills, low pressure, discolored water, slow drains, sewer odors and warm spots on floors are all good reasons. Homes with original plumbing also benefit from one.

    A general home inspector checks basic function, while a plumber goes deeper with sewer cameras, pressure tests and pipe identification. Plenty of buyers use both.

    We can include estimated repair ranges in the report and firm quotes if you want us to do the work. There is no obligation to hire us for repairs.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. Damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell under a sink or a warm patch of tile can all point to a hidden leak that an inspection will find before it does more damage.
