    What Goes Into a Water Heater Swap in Durham, NH

    Bradford White, Rheem and AO Smith in Durham, NH

    We install Bradford White, Rheem and AO Smith on the majority of our swaps.     Attic Water Heater Replacement in Durham

    The housing stock in New Hampshire includes many old farmhouses and village homes, often with cast iron drains and galvanized supply lines. Attic water heaters take more work to replace than ones in a garage or closet. Getting a 50-gallon tank up a pull-down ladder or through a narrow scuttle takes two people and care, and code calls for a drain pan with a line to the outside or a safe drain, a service platform and a light near the unit. Two-person crew, always.

    Electric Water Heater Replacement in Durham

    Electric water heaters in Durham usually fail from a burned-out heating element, a bad thermostat or a tank that has finally rusted through at the bottom. Elements are cheap to swap. A leaking tank cannot, so if water is coming from the tank body itself, we replace the heater and match the voltage, wattage and breaker size to what your panel already supplies. Most swaps take a few hours.

    Commercial Water Heater Replacement in Durham

    Businesses in Durham put far more hours on a heater than a house, so commercial replacements need more capacity and faster recovery. Restaurants, salons and offices all draw differently, so we size by peak gallons per hour and required temperature, which may mean a larger tank, a booster or a tankless rack. Downtime costs money.

    Water Heater Replacement Cost Comparison in Durham, NH

    A standard tank replacement costs the least up front in Durham, NH. Tankless and hybrid heat pump models cost more to buy and install but can cut operating costs over the life of the unit, and tankless units often last 15 to 20 years compared to roughly 8 to 12 for a typical tank. Ask for each option.

    Upsizing Your Water Heater in Durham

    Running out of hot water usually means the house outgrew the tank. A bigger tank or a higher first-hour rating handles more back-to-back showers, and sometimes a 50-gallon high-recovery unit matches a 75 in the same footprint. Recovery rate counts too.
