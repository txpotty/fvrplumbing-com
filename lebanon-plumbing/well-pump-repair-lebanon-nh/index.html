    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Lebanon, NH

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Lebanon

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. FVR Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Lebanon, NH well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Lebanon

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Lebanon, NH well.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Lebanon

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. Shaft seal leaks and worn impellers come next. The pump gets reprimed and resealed, and the foot valve and suction line get checked, because a single air leak will undo the work. Everything is above ground.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Lebanon

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. Precharge gets checked on an empty tank. It should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure, so a 40/60 switch wants roughly 38 psi in the tank.

    Jet Pump Repair in Lebanon, NH

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Lost prime, seal leaks and impeller wear account for most jet pump calls. Our crew in Lebanon restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Lebanon, NH

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. FVR Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and confirms it builds pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.

    No-Water Emergency Repair in Lebanon

    A dead pump leaves the house without toilets, showers or drinking water. FVR Plumbing answers emergency well pump calls throughout Lebanon and nearby areas and puts them ahead of routine work. Power and the pressure switch get checked first. Those two items fix a surprising share of no-water calls without anyone having to touch the pump hanging down in the well.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Lebanon

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, a clogged sensing tube and ant nests are the usual reasons one fails. After swapping the switch and clearing the tube, we set cut-in and cut-out to match the tank, usually 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.
