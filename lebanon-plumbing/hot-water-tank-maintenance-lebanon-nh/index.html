    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Lebanon

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. Those chunks cause the popping. When a plain flush will not move them, we use a descaling solution rated for potable systems, let it sit and then flush it all out. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Expansion Tank Service in Lebanon, NH

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. We test the air side with a tire gauge. Precharge should match your house water pressure. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Lebanon, NH

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. We pull the burner assembly and brush it clean. We also check the thermocouple or flame sensor, since a weak one lets the pilot go out every few days and leaves you resetting it on your knees. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Lebanon, NH

    A maintenance plan puts your tank on the calendar so nobody has to remember. You get a reminder call when the flush is due.     Relief Valve Testing in Lebanon

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Sediment Flushing in Lebanon, NH

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. We flush with full line pressure, opening and closing the cold supply in bursts to stir the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Lebanon, NH

Swapping a $40 to $80 rod is a lot cheaper than a new tank, and on a tank under eight years old it is usually the best money you can spend on it.
