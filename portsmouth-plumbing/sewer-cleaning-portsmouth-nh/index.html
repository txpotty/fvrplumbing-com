    Preventative Sewer Maintenance in Portsmouth

    Roots come back after cutting. Usually within a year or so. Putting a root-prone main on a yearly cleaning schedule keeps the growth small enough that it never stops the line, and it costs far less than an after-hours emergency call.

    Hydro Jetting for Sewer Lines in Portsmouth

    Jetting cleans the whole pipe wall. A cable only makes a path. Water at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI strips grease, scale and fine roots off the inside of the line and flushes it all toward the street, which leaves the pipe close to its original diameter.

    Grease and Scale Buildup Cleaning in Portsmouth, NH

    Grease bonds with mineral scale into a hard crust. Water in much of New Hampshire is soft and often slightly acidic, which can slowly corrode copper pipe and cause pinhole leaks. Flow slows a little each year. A cable can punch a hole through the crust, but a jetter cleans the walls back to full size and leaves less for new grease to grab onto, and the method depends on pipe material and condition.

    Root Removal From Sewer Lines in Portsmouth

    Trees send roots toward any moisture they can find. Leaky joints feed them all year. We use a sectional cable with a root saw sized to the pipe to cut the mass free and pull it out, then follow with a jetter if fine root hairs are left behind, and a camera shows where roots are entering so you can choose between lining and repair.

    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Portsmouth, NH

    Grease hardens as it cools. It shrinks the opening a little every week. Kitchen grease coats the sewer line the same way plaque coats an artery, and over a few years the opening can close to the size of a finger, which is when drains start to back up, and jetting is the right fix.

    Root Masses in Portsmouth Sewer Laterals

    Mature trees and old clay laterals do not mix. Roots find the joints. We cut roots in several passes, then look on camera to judge the damage and talk about lining or a spot repair.
