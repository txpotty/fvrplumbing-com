    Perma-Liner and NuFlow Trenchless Systems in Portsmouth

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow both make lining systems we use, each with its own resin chemistry and installation equipment. Both are built to ASTM standards for cured-in-place pipe. The brand matters less than the prep, because a liner bonded to a dirty, scaly pipe wall will not hold the way the manufacturer rates it. We clean to bare pipe first.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Portsmouth, NH

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. We wet out a felt tube with epoxy resin, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Cure time runs a few hours. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Perma-Liner Lining Equipment in Portsmouth

    Perma-Liner makes inversion drums and epoxy systems for lining 2- to 6-inch drain lines, which covers most of what we see in houses. The equipment fits in a pickup bed. Compact gear matters on tight lots. Resin gets mixed on site and timed to the length of the run, so it does not start curing before the liner is in place.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Prep and Lining in Portsmouth

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. Skip that step and the liner can wrinkle or fail to bond. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Sewer Camera Inspection for Trenchless Repair in Portsmouth

    Every trenchless job starts with a push camera fed in from the cleanout or a pulled toilet. The monitor shows us the pipe wall in real time, along with the footage counter, and a sonde in the camera head lets us mark the exact spot on the ground above it. That tells us the depth. No guessing where to dig.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Repair in Portsmouth

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. Over time it softens, goes oval and blisters on the inside, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Clay that still holds its round shape is a good candidate for a liner.
