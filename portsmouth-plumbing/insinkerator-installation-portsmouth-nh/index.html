    Badger Series Installation in Portsmouth, NH

    The Badger line runs from the 1/3 HP Badger 1 up to the Badger 5XP. All of them use the same quick-lock mount. That shared mount is why a Badger-to-Badger swap is often a 45-minute job, since the upper ring and flange can stay put if they are not corroded. No cord comes in the box, so the cord kit is on us.

    Badger Disposal Install in Portsmouth

    Most replacement disposals in local kitchens are a Badger. A 1/3 to 3/4 HP Badger handles normal scraps and fits almost any sink base cabinet. We hang it on the quick-lock mount, connect the discharge tube and add a cord kit if the old unit was plugged in, then run a test grind with cold water running to clear the chamber. About an hour, usually.

    Evolution Series Installation in Portsmouth

    Evolution units grind in two or three stages and carry extra sound insulation around the motor housing, which is why they sit taller and weigh more than a Badger. Clearance under the bowl matters here. Before install day we measure the drop from sink to trap arm so the unit fits. Tight cabinets suit the Compact, while the Excel wants the most space.

    First-Time Disposal Installation in Portsmouth

    Kitchens that never had a disposal need some rework. First, the sink gets a disposal flange. The tailpiece and trap have to be reworked, and the cabinet needs a switched outlet, which means an electrician if there is no power under the sink already. Drain slope to the stack gets checked as well.

    Evolution Disposal Upgrades in Portsmouth, NH

    Stringy food is where these units earn their price. Corn husks and celery will stall a light disposal. An Evolution Compact or Excel grinds in stages and has more torque, so those scraps break into small pieces instead of wrapping around the impellers and tripping the overload. We check cabinet clearance first.

    Standard InSinkErator Installation in Portsmouth

    A standard install goes into a sink that already has a drain and power. The old disposal comes down, the flange gets scraped clean and a new one goes in on fresh putty. Then the mount ring goes on. We hang the unit, turn the lower ring until it locks and connect the discharge tube to the trap with a new slip-joint washer so nothing weeps. Power comes from a cord kit or the existing hardwire, checked for ground before the breaker goes back on.
