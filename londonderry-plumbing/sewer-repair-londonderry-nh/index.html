    Sewer Line Backups and Blockages in Londonderry

    Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains is a classic main line symptom. The clog is past the branches. Older New Hampshire homes with clay lines often show roots right at the joints. We record what we find.

    Sewer Line Hydro-Jetting in Londonderry

    A hydro-jetter pushes water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scours the full inside of the pipe. A cable only punches a hole. That makes jetting the better choice for grease, scale and fine root growth, and we always jet a line before lining it. Brittle or broken pipe is a different story. We check the pipe on video before we jet it.

    Tree Root Invasion Solutions in Londonderry

    Roots in a sewer line are not the tree's fault. The pipe was leaking first. Warm, wet, nutrient-rich air escaping from a loose joint draws roots toward the line, and once they get in they thicken until they crack the pipe or separate the joint completely. We cut them out, then talk about the long-term fix. A liner or new pipe at that joint is what actually stops regrowth.

    Restaurant and Retail Sewer Repair in Londonderry

    Restaurants, retail centers and multifamily buildings load a lateral far beyond a single house. Grease is the usual culprit. We jet the line, camera it, and repair the weak section with options that limit how long you are closed, including after-hours digs and bypass pumping when the business has to stay open. We also install two-way cleanouts for future maintenance.

    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Londonderry

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. It flattens with age. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, so by the time a camera shows widespread damage, spot repairs are a poor investment. PVC goes in, usually end to end. Trenchless bursting is often an option.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Londonderry

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. A fully collapsed section is one example. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. We see this most in older neighborhoods with clay pipe. The dig is staged so the house is without a sewer for as little time as possible.

    Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Londonderry, NH

    Trenchless repair means we fix the pipe without opening a trench along its full length. Two access holes, sometimes one. Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE line through the old one and splits the old pipe outward, while cured-in-place lining pushes a resin-soaked felt tube into the old pipe and hardens it into a new wall. Either way, the driveway and the trees stay put. Not every line qualifies, and we will say so.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. The tub often shows it first.     Digging up and replacing a lateral means trenching near gas and electric lines. You will be working in sewage.     Schedule 40 PVC is our standard for open trench work. PVC will not corrode. For pipe bursting we use fused HDPE, which comes in continuous lengths with no joints for roots to find, while lining jobs use epoxy-saturated tubes built for long service life. Local code decides the final choice.

    Watch for clogs that keep coming back in more than one drain. Toilets that gurgle count too.
