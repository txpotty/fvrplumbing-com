    Faucet and Disposal Hookup in Exeter, NH

    New sinks often get a new disposal. We mount the disposal on a fresh flange with putty, connect the dishwasher knockout so the hose drains through it, and confirm the outlet sits above the trap inlet so it drains properly. Forgetting the knockout plug floods the dishwasher. We run both before leaving.

    Leak-Proof Sink Connections in Exeter, NH

    Every joint gets tested before we leave. We fill the bowl to the overflow, pull the stopper, and watch every nut under the cabinet at full flow with a flashlight and a dry paper towel. A towel shows a weep fast. Pipe dope goes on threads, not compression fittings.

    Undermount Sink Installation in Exeter

    Undermount sinks hang from the countertop. We set the bowl with the clips or anchors the stone shop provides, run a continuous bead of 100 percent silicone around the rim, and leave it braced while the adhesive cures overnight. Adhesive by itself will not hold it. Plumbing goes on the next day.

    Pedestal Sink Mounting in Exeter

    The pedestal is mostly for looks. A wall bracket lagged into blocking carries the weight, and if the wall has none, we open it and add a board. Drywall anchors do not cut it. A loose basin cracks the trap.

    Vessel Sink Installation in Exeter, NH

    Vessel sinks sit on top of the counter. That raises the rim several inches, so we either pick a taller vessel faucet or mount a wall faucet, and we use a vessel drain that matches the bowl's overflow or lack of one. No overflow means no slotted drain. Getting that wrong leaves water under the bowl.

    Undermount Clips and Cure Time in Exeter

    Undermount bowls need overnight to cure. Silicone holds in a few hours but needs about a day before it carries a full bowl. The bowl stays braced until our return. Plumbing goes in on the second visit.
