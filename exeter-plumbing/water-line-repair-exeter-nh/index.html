    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Exeter

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Exeter

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. Then the new pipe gets pulled through underground. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Exeter

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Rocks pressing against the pipe wear pinholes over time, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. A two-foot section is typical. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, then we look over the pipe on either side for more pitting before we backfill.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Exeter, NH

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in Exeter, NH

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. A break shuts the business down. FVR Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Exeter, NH

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patches on those materials rarely hold. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, usually in one day with a trench or a trenchless pull. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Exeter, NH

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Fittings in the box loosen with ground movement and traffic. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, because the water provider owns the meter and everything on the street side. When it is on your side, we repair it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Exeter

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Newer homes typically use copper or PEX supply lines with plastic drains, which hold up better against corrosive water. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Exeter

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. A shallow spot repair is cheap. Most single-leak repairs land between $300 and $800, while a full main line replacement can run $1,500 to $6,000 or more depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, while the new pipe is pulled into the path it just opened. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, which most residential service lines are.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, 811 marking, safe digging around the gas and power lines nearby and code-approved fittings made up correctly. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Spot repairs are often exempt. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Copper is Type K for buried runs. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, and we stick with fittings rated for direct burial in the soil conditions we find. Off-brand fittings stay off the truck.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. Replacements usually take a day. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, though digging the two pits and setting up the boring equipment takes a few hours on its own before any pipe moves. In most cases your water is back on before evening.
